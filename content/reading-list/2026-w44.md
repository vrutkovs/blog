  When parts of a software system rely on incompatible assumptions, every change takes more work. AI-generated code can accelerate that buildup, while agents cannot reliably repair it without real-world feedback and human understanding.

# Tech Industry & Culture

* [Slouching towards Bethlehem](https://deadsimpletech.com/blog/slouching-to-bethlehem)

  We find ourselves at the precipice before the break. There's enough instability to see how things are going: things are breaking, vital things are ceasing to work, the bulk of the population hates what the industry is doing to them. The best are exhausted and isolated, while the worst are creating yet another vibe-coded pile of crap.

  Tags: Tech, Crisis, Optimism, Modernism, Revelation

* [25th October 2026](https://samthursfield.wordpress.com/2026/10/25/25th-october-2026/)

  After 22 years of blogging, Sam Thursfield revisits a PHP wiki he built on SourceForge. An intrusion in 2003 felt harmless compared with the attacks it would face today; the experience also shows how much hacker culture has changed.

  Tags: PHP, Wiki, Security, OpenSource, Hacker

* [Don't think](https://deadsimpletech.com/blog/dont-think)

  The tech-adjacent anglosphere, while it doesn't have secret police brutalising dissidents in *my* country just yet (though I must stress that the USA is definitely blurring into that kind of direction), actually makes more claims on the internal forum. It's not enough to desultorily go to the May Day parade once a year and have your "Workers of the  world, unite!" poster in the window, and it's not enough to simply not think or keep your thinking to yourself, do your work and go along. No, you have to think, and you have to think correctly.

  Tags: Thought, Society, Status, Innovation, Control

* [The /r/opensource Rule Changes](https://danb.me/blog/opensource-sub-rules/?ref=selfh.st)

  New rules on /r/opensource restrict AI content, project posts, and non-English submissions. A moderator explains why he disagrees with the changes and is leaving the team.

  Tags: Reddit, OpenSource, AI, Rule, Moderation

* [The virtue of programming is diligence](https://deadsimpletech.com/blog/virtue-programming-diligence)

  Clearly there's something importance about the practice of diligence that makes for excellent software engineers. In fact, I'd tend to go further: diligence is the cardinal virtue of software work, and our first priority when doing anything to do with tech should be to work diligently.

  Tags: Diligence, Programming, Virtue, Ethic, Work

# Software Engineering & Cloud

* [Initiative: A Self-Hosted Workspace for Groups](https://noted.lol/initiative/)

  Initiative gives groups a shared workspace for projects, task boards, and collaborative documents. It supports multiple communities with separate permissions and runs on your own server with Docker Compose and PostgreSQL.

  Tags: Initiative, Self-host, Project, Workspace, Community

* [VictoriaLogs vs Loki, Elasticsearch, OpenSearch, SigNoz and ClickStack on structured log ingestion](https://victoriametrics.com/blog/structured-log-ingestion-benchmark/index.html)

  A single-node Kubernetes benchmark compares six systems on structured log ingestion rate, CPU, memory, disk use, and query speed. VictoriaLogs balances ingestion and resource use; Loki ingests fastest but uses more memory and queries slowly.

  Tags: Benchmark, Log, Performance, Storage, System
